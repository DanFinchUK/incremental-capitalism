What these runs assume

Every figure comes from the pacing sim (npm run sim:solo) playing the real game. Only the game's random draws are fixed, so one thing can be measured at a time; the bot never does anything a player couldn't.

  • Heir and inherited traits: every heir the game drafts carries the trait being measured. The draft still happens and the bot still picks an heir.
  • Advisors: every introduction offers only the advisor being measured. Introductions still come when ordeals are won, the bot takes the advisor or turns them down, and the office empties at retirement as usual. In Rankings it holds one of the family at a time; in Copies it takes them until it holds that many, and held shows how many it really had.
  • Baseline: heirs with no traits, and every introduction turned down.
  • Worth reads the time saved against runs with all income multiplied by 1.1, 1.25, 1.5, 2 and 3. That multiplier isn't in the game: it is the ruler, so worth x2 saves as much time as doubling all income would. Time saved is against the baseline, seed by seed, with one standard error beside it.

The bot plays better than most people, and that shapes what it can value:

  • It taps five times a second throughout and buys the best return per pound every two seconds, so buyers and the gentry traits that bring the family's own staff save it nothing.
  • It starts an ordeal only when its sums say it will comfortably win, so it is never disgraced and longer ordeals or softer twists don't help it.
  • It never leaves, so offline earnings never count, and it pays the first answer to every scandal.
  • It retires after 12 minutes without a new class, buys perks in a set order and picks the rarest heir and advisor on offer.
